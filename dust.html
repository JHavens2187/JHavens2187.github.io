Constraining Dust Attenuation Curves in Star-Forming Galaxies

Breaking the fundamental degeneracy between dust normalization ($\tau_V$) and attenuation slope ($n$) from $1 < z < 7$ using a multi-line hydrogen recombination framework.

INSTRUMENTATION & TOOLS
JWST / NIRSpec JADES Survey Chandra / CSC2.1 Python

The Physics Problem

Traditional approaches based on the Balmer decrement constrain only the overall attenuation normalization and require an assumed attenuation curve shape. To resolve this, we apply a multi-line hydrogen recombination framework to constrain both the normalization and slope of the dust attenuation law for star-forming galaxies.

The Dynamic Solution: Using JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy from the JADES surveys, we combine measurements of $H\alpha$, $H\beta$, and a third hydrogen line—preferring near-infrared Paschen transitions ($Pa\alpha$, $Pa\beta$, or $Pa\gamma$). This extended wavelength baseline breaks the degeneracy, allowing us to jointly solve for the attenuation parameters $\tau_V$ and $n$ using a multi-start Newton-Raphson numerical implementation.

Hierarchical AGN Veto Pipeline

Because the multi-line decrement method assumes intrinsic line ratios governed strictly by Case B recombination in HII regions, any contamination from Broad-Line or Narrow-Line Region emission artificially skews the observed line ratios. Starting from a base sample of 5,215 galaxies, we isolate a pristine baseline using rigorous multi-wavelength indicators:

  • Hard Indicators (AGN): Galaxies are completely excluded if cross-matched with Chandra Source Catalog 2.1 data showing an X-ray luminosity of $L_X > 10^{42}$ erg s$^{-1}$.
  • Soft Indicators (AGN Candidates): Sources are flagged and excluded if they simultaneously trigger broad-line kinematics (FWHM > 1000 km s$^{-1}$) and highly ionized optical line ratios on the OHNO diagnostic diagram.
  • Final Sample: Filtering leaves a pure, high-fidelity star-forming baseline of 5,165 galaxies.

Numerical Convergence & Dust Evolution

Of the pure star-forming sample passed to the numerical solver, 192 galaxies successfully converged to physically allowed attenuation solutions, yielding broad ranges of both normalizations ($\tau_V$) and slopes ($n$).

Redshift Evolution: The recovered parameters demonstrate that only 30.7% of galaxies fall within traditionally adopted slope ranges ($n=0.7-1.3$). Furthermore, high-redshift systems ($z > 3.5$) are best fit by systematically steeper attenuation slopes ($n \approx 2.2$) and lower optical depths ($\tau_V \approx 0.3$). This indicates that galaxies at earlier cosmic times possess a larger fraction of small dust grains, lower metallicities, and simpler star-dust geometries.

Closing the Mathematical System

Fixed-slope methods, such as the local starburst law (Calzetti, $n=0.7$) or the SMC bar extinction law ($n=1.3$), systematically fail when applied across broad wavelength baselines. By forcing a shallow optical slope to reproduce profound optical reddening, these models drastically overestimate residual infrared opacity, applying an exponentially inflated correction that artificially blows up near-infrared Star Formation Rates (SFR).

Conversely, treating the power-law index $n$ as a free parameter introduces the necessary mathematical degree of freedom to reconcile the optical and near-infrared baseline. Applying our dynamically derived attenuation parameters produces a tight mathematical closure across transitions, settling at a highly consistent +0.008 dex median SFR ratio.
